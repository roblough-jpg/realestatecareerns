Some of my agents work on teams and some work on their own. Both can work well. The right choice depends on where you are in your career and what you need most right now.

## What's the difference between a real estate team and a solo agent?

A team is a group of agents working under a team lead, inside a brokerage. The lead usually supplies leads, systems or marketing, and takes a share of the team's commissions in return.

A solo agent works directly with the brokerage. You find your own clients, run your own marketing and keep whatever your brokerage split allows.

Either way, you're licensed under a brokerage, and your broker is responsible for supervising your work.

## The case for joining a team

- **You learn faster.** You see more deals sooner, and someone experienced is beside you for the hard parts.
- **Leads.** A good team brings in business you couldn't find on your own in year one.
- **Support.** Admin help, showing coverage and someone to call when an offer goes sideways.

## The case for going solo

- **You keep more of each deal.** No team share comes off the top.
- **Your name, your brand.** Your past clients and your reputation are clearly yours.
- **Control.** You choose your hours, your marketing and the kind of clients you work with.

## Who does better where?

- **New agents with a small network** often do better on a strong team for a year or two.
- **Agents with a steady base of past clients and referrals** often do better solo.
- **Part-time agents** can benefit from a team's showing coverage, if the team is open to part-time members.

## Questions to ask before you join a team

- What's the split on team leads, and what's the split on my own clients?
- How many leads do agents actually get each month, and how are they shared out?
- What training and coaching will I get, and from whom?
- Whose name goes on the signs and ads?
- What happens to my clients and leads if I leave the team?
