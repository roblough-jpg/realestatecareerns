Part-time real estate sounds like the safe way in. Keep your job, sell a few homes on the side, and see how it goes. It can work, but you should go in knowing what it really takes.

## Can I be a part-time real estate agent in Nova Scotia?

Yes. A licence doesn't come with a minimum number of hours. What matters is whether your brokerage accepts part-time agents and whether you can be there for your clients when they need you.

## What's hard about part-time real estate?

- **Clients set the schedule.** Buyers want to see homes on evenings and weekends, and offers come with deadlines. A new listing doesn't wait until your shift ends.
- **The fixed costs don't shrink.** Your licence, memberships, insurance and continuing education cost the same whether you close two deals or twenty.
- **Slower growth.** Most business comes from staying in touch with people. Less time usually means fewer conversations and fewer deals.
- **Your clients still deserve full service.** Paperwork, FINTRAC identification and deadlines have to be right on every deal, part-time or not.

## When does part-time make sense?

- **As a bridge.** You keep your income while you build savings and your first few clients.
- **When your other job is flexible.** Shift work or a set schedule with free daytime blocks can fit well.
- **With a backup plan for showings.** Some agents work with a partner or team who can cover when they can't.

Set a date to decide. "Part-time for the first year, then full-time if I've closed X deals" is a plan. "Part-time until it takes off" usually isn't.

## Questions to ask a brokerage about part-time

- Do you accept part-time agents, and do you expect anything different from them?
- What do I pay in a month when I don't sell anything?
- Who covers a showing or an offer if I'm at my other job?
- Is training offered at times I can attend?
