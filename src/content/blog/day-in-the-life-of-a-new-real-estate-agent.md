People picture showings and closing day. Those happen, but they're a small part of a new agent's week. Here's what a typical day looks like in your first year, based on what I see my newer agents do.

## What does a new real estate agent do all day?

No two days are the same, but a good one looks something like this.

- **Morning: reach out.** Calls, texts and messages to people you know, past contacts and new leads. This is the most important hour of the day and the easiest one to skip.
- **Late morning: learn.** Brokerage training, reviewing forms, studying the market in your area, and watching how experienced agents handle a deal.
- **Midday: admin and follow-up.** Answer messages, update your contact list, prepare for appointments and get your paperwork right.
- **Afternoon: preview and prepare.** Look at homes before you show them, pull comparable sales, prepare for a listing appointment.
- **Evening: clients.** Showings, buyer meetings and offers often happen after people finish work.

## What surprises new agents most?

- **How much of the job is follow-up.** Most business comes from staying in touch, not from one big break.
- **How slowly the money comes.** You're paid at closing, often 30 to 60 days after an accepted offer.
- **How much paperwork there is.** Every deal has forms, deadlines and FINTRAC identification rules that have to be right.
- **How quiet some days are.** A slow day is a reach-out day. The agents who keep calling through the quiet weeks are the ones who get busy.

## What does a busy day look like later?

As your client list grows, the balance shifts. More of your day goes to showings, negotiating offers, inspections and keeping deals on track. Reaching out still comes first, because it's what fills next season.

## How do I make the first year work?

- **Guard your morning.** Do your calls before anything else.
- **Track your activity.** Conversations lead to appointments, which lead to deals. Count them.
- **Ask for help early.** A good brokerage wants you to call before something becomes a problem.
