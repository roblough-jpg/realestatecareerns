I've hired and trained a lot of new agents. The ones who struggle usually weren't short on talent. They just didn't know what the job really asked of them before they signed up. This self-check is the conversation I'd have with you across the desk.

## The ten-question self-check

Answer each one honestly. Count your clear "yes" answers.

1. **Can I live on savings or another income for six months or more?** Commission arrives only when a deal closes.
2. **Am I comfortable telling the people I know that I'm in real estate, and asking them to keep me in mind?** Most first deals come from people who already know you.
3. **Will I make calls and follow up even when nobody is checking on me?** No one schedules your day for you.
4. **Am I OK working evenings and weekends?** That's when buyers are free to see homes.
5. **Can I handle someone else's stress without taking it on?** Buying or selling a home is emotional. Your calm is part of the service.
6. **Do I like paperwork, or at least not hate it?** Every deal comes with forms, deadlines and identification rules that have to be right.
7. **Can I hear "no" a lot and keep going?** Most conversations won't turn into business, at least not right away.
8. **Am I willing to keep learning?** Markets, rules and forms change, and licensed agents complete continuing education.
9. **Do I have the money to get started?** The course, licence, memberships and insurance add up before you earn a dollar.
10. **Do I actually enjoy helping people with big decisions?** That's the job, every day.

**8 to 10 yeses:** you're seeing the job clearly. Start planning.
**5 to 7:** it can work, but fix the "no" answers first, especially money and follow-up.
**4 or fewer:** take a hard look before you pay for the course. That's not a failure. It may save you a lot of money.

## Is being a real estate agent a good career? The honest pros

- **You build something that's yours.** Your reputation and your list of past clients go with you for your whole career.
- **Your income isn't capped by a salary grid.** It also isn't guaranteed, which is the other side of the same coin.
- **Flexible, not easy.** You decide when to prospect and when to take a morning off. You also answer the phone at dinner when an offer comes in.
- **Real work that matters to people.** Helping a family into their first home is a good day.

## And the honest cons

- **Commission only.** No sales, no pay. Job Bank lists a Nova Scotia median of about $51,600 a year for real estate agents, but the range runs from about $36,000 to $152,000, and first-year agents usually sit at the low end. I break this down in [how much realtors make in Nova Scotia](/blog/how-much-do-realtors-make-in-nova-scotia/).
- **Slow start.** It often takes a few months before your first deal closes and you're paid.
- **Upfront and yearly costs.** In 2026 the online licensing course alone is about $3,450, before the licence, memberships and insurance. Try the [licensing cost calculator](/tools/licensing-cost-calculator/) for your own number.
- **It's a business, not a job.** Marketing, bookkeeping and setting money aside for tax are on you.

## How long does it take to find out if it's for you?

Getting licensed usually takes six to twelve months from start to finish. The [Century 21 Optimum guide to how long it takes](https://optimumrealty.c21.ca/how-long-does-it-take-to-become-a-realtor-in-nova-scotia) walks through each stage. After that, give yourself a full year before you judge the career. The first year is when you're building the habits and the list that pay off later.
