FINTRAC paperwork isn't the exciting part of a deal, but it's the part regulators check. I want every agent at our brokerage to know these rules cold, so here is the plain-language version, taken from FINTRAC's own guidance.

## When do I have to verify a client's identity?

- **Every time you act for a buyer or seller.** Verify your client's identity when you act as their agent in a purchase or sale.
- **When you receive funds.** For any amount, verify the person's identity at the time of the transaction. For a corporation or other entity, you have 30 days.
- **When you receive $10,000 or more in cash or virtual currency** in a single transaction, or in several within 24 hours.
- **For an unrepresented party.** If the other side has no agent, you keep an information record for them, and that means verifying their identity too.

You must use the identification methods FINTRAC prescribes, and record what you checked.

## What records do I have to keep?

| Record | When it's needed | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information record | Every client you act for, plus any unrepresented party | Name, address, date of birth and occupation (for an entity: name, address, principal business) |
| Receipt of funds record | Any funds you receive, any amount | Date, who paid, amount and how much was cash, how it was received, purpose, accounts and reference numbers |
| Large cash transaction record and report | $10,000 or more in cash | Who, how much, purpose, how received, and more |
| Large virtual currency record and report | $10,000 or more in virtual currency | Who, how much, the currency, exchange rate and transaction identifiers |

Records are kept for at least five years and must be produced within 30 days if FINTRAC asks.

## What else does FINTRAC expect?

- **Beneficial ownership.** When your client is a company or other entity, find out who owns or controls 25% or more of it, and take reasonable measures to confirm it.
- **Third party determination.** For certain records and reports, find out whether someone else is behind the transaction.
- **Politically exposed persons.** Take reasonable measures to find out whether a client is a politically exposed person or head of an international organization, when the rules call for it.
- **Ongoing monitoring.** Keep client information up to date based on risk.
- **Suspicious transactions.** If something doesn't add up, report it to FINTRAC as soon as practicable, whatever the amount. This one is your responsibility as well as your broker's.

## Who's responsible: me or my broker?

If you work for a brokerage, your broker is responsible for most of it, including the compliance program: a compliance officer, written policies, a risk assessment, training, and an effectiveness review every two years. Suspicious transaction reporting is the exception. It sits with you and your broker.
