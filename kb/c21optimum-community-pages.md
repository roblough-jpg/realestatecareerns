## Halifax (/neighbourhoods/halifax-ns)

Halifax is the capital of Nova Scotia and the heart of the region, on the western side of one of the world's great natural harbours. The peninsula holds the downtown, the universities and the hospitals, while the mainland side stretches out to Clayton Park, Fairview, Rockingham, Armdale and Spryfield.

Downtown and the peninsula are among the most walkable places in the province. The 4 km waterfront boardwalk runs past shops, restaurants and the ferry terminal, and Citadel Hill, the Halifax Public Gardens, the Halifax Common and Point Pleasant Park are all within reach on foot or by bike.

Getting around is straightforward. The Halifax-Dartmouth ferry, the oldest continuously running saltwater ferry in North America, crosses the harbour, and the Macdonald and MacKay bridges connect to Dartmouth. Halifax Transit covers the whole city, and Highway 102 leads to Bedford, Sackville and the airport.

Halifax is home to Dalhousie University, Saint Mary's University, Mount Saint Vincent University and NSCAD, which keeps rental demand strong around the campuses. Families have a wide choice of public schools; confirm the catchment for a specific address with the Halifax Regional Centre for Education.

Homes range from heritage houses and character flats in the South End and North End, to condos downtown and along the waterfront, to family neighbourhoods and newer developments on the mainland. Buying an older home on the peninsula? Plan for a thorough inspection: knob-and-tube wiring, older plumbing and stone foundations are common in houses of that age.

## Dartmouth (/neighbourhoods/dartmouth-ns)

Dartmouth sits on the eastern shore of Halifax Harbour, directly across from downtown Halifax. Founded in 1750, it became a city in 1961 and joined the Halifax Regional Municipality in 1996. It is known as the City of Lakes, with more than twenty lakes inside its old boundaries.

The lakes shape daily life here. Lake Banook is home to canoe and kayak clubs, Sullivan's Pond is a favourite for a walk, and Shubie Park follows the historic Shubenacadie Canal with trails, a campground and a beach on Lake Charles.

Commuting is easy in any direction. The Alderney and Woodside ferries cross to downtown Halifax, the Macdonald and MacKay bridges are minutes away, and Highways 111 and 118 connect to Burnside, Dartmouth Crossing and the airport.

Shopping includes Mic Mac Mall and Dartmouth Crossing, while Downtown Dartmouth has grown into a lively spot for independent shops, cafes and restaurants near the ferry terminal. Families have plenty of school choice; confirm the catchment for a specific address with the Halifax Regional Centre for Education.

Homes range from older character houses near the downtown and Lake Banook, to established family neighbourhoods such as Woodlawn, Portland Estates and Colby Village, to newer condos and townhouses. Looking at a lakefront home? Ask about shoreline rules and any septic or water details before you write an offer.

## Sackville (/neighbourhoods/sackville-ns)

Sackville sits north of Bedford, about 27 km from downtown Halifax, where Highways 101 and 102 meet. It is one of the most established family communities in the municipality, with Lower Sackville at its centre and Middle Sackville, Upper Sackville and Beaverbank nearby.

Sackville Drive is the main street, lined with grocery stores, restaurants and everyday services. Halifax Transit runs from the Sackville and Cobequid terminals, and the highways put Halifax, Dartmouth, Burnside and Halifax Stanfield International Airport within easy reach.

For recreation, the Sackville Sports Stadium has pools, a gym and a curling club, and First Lake and Sackville Lakes Provincial Park offer swimming, trails and green space. The Bedford-Sackville Connector Greenway follows the Sackville River all the way to Bedford.

Families are served by local elementary and junior high schools, with Sackville High School and Millwood High School at the high school level. Always confirm the catchment for a specific address with the Halifax Regional Centre for Education.

Sackville is known for good value. Housing is mostly detached homes, split-levels and bungalows on good-sized lots, plus townhouses and newer subdivisions on the edges of the community. Many homes date from the 1970s and 1980s, so check the age of the roof, windows and heating system and budget for updates.
